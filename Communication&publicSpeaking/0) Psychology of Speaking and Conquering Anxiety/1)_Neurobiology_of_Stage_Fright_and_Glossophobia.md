# 1) Neurobiology of Stage Fright and Glossophobia

## Executive Overview
Glossophobia (the fear of public speaking) affects up to 75% of the population. From an evolutionary perspective, human survival historically depended on group acceptance; social evaluation was interpreted by the brain as life-or-death vulnerability. When stepping in front of an audience or a C-level boardroom, the brain's survival machinery engages automatically.

## 2. Physiological Manifestations & Mechanics

| Symptom | Underlying Physiological Mechanism | Speaker Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Racing Heart (Tachycardia)** | Epinephrine activates $\beta_1$-adrenergic receptors on cardiac pacemaker cells. | Perceived panic, shortness of breath, trembling voice. |
| **Shaky Hands & Trembling Knees** | Blood shunted from fine motor neurons to large muscle groups (fight-or-flight). | Inability to hold a laser pointer or notes without shaking. |
| **Dry Mouth (Xerostomia)** | Parasympathetic salivary gland inhibition via sympathetic dominance. | Sticky tongue, clicking sounds into microphone, tight throat. |
| **High-Pitched / Strained Voice** | Laryngeal and cricothyroid muscle spasm under acute cortisol elevation. | Voice cracks, sounds defensive, nervous, or infantile. |
| **Brain Fog / Memory Loss** | Prefrontal cortex inhibition by amygdalar override; working memory drops. | Forgetting slides, losing train of thought mid-sentence. |

---

## 3. The 3-Phase Defense Timeline

1. **Anticipatory Spike (T-minus 24h to 10m)**:
   - Rumination, insomnia, catastrophizing (*"What if they ask a question I can't answer?"*).
2. **The Initial Shock (First 60 seconds on stage)**:
   - Peak autonomic surge. Heart rate peaks; respiration rate doubles. This is where 90% of speakers falter, speak too fast, or stammer.
3. **The Habituation Plateau (Minute 3 onwards)**:
   - If the speaker survives the first 90 seconds without panic runaway, parasympathetic tone gradually reasserts itself, heart rate stabilizes, and prefrontal cognitive bandwidth returns.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways
- Stage fright is not a character defect; it is a hardwired autonomic reflex.
- You cannot eliminate epinephrine release through sheer willpower; you must use **somatic overrides** (breath, vagal nerve stimulation) to force parasympathetic braking.
- The battle for composure is won or lost in the **first 90 seconds** of speech delivery.
