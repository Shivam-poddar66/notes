# 3) Cognitive Reappraisal and the Service Mindset

## Executive Overview
While somatic techniques regulate the physical body, cognitive framing dictates how your brain interprets autonomic arousal. By transforming threat appraisals into challenge/excitement states, you maintain cognitive agility and connection.

---

## 1. Arousal Reappraisal: "I am Excited"

```text
       TRADITIONAL ADVICE (FLAWED)                   HARVARD REAPPRAISAL PROTOCOL
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┐          ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ "Just calm down and relax"        │          │ "I am excited / I am eager"       │
   │                                   │          │                                   │
   │ High Arousal ──X──► Low Arousal   │          │ High Arousal ──────► High Arousal │
   │ (Biochemically Impossible in 10s) │          │ (Negative Threat ──► Opportunity) │
   └───────────────────────────────────┘          └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Science (Alison Wood Brooks, Harvard Business School)
- Both anxiety and excitement share high autonomic arousal: elevated cardiac output, cortisol surge, and heightened sensory alertness.
- Attempting to shift from high arousal (anxiety) to low arousal (calmness) creates massive cognitive dissonance.
- Saying out loud, *"I am excited to share this solution"* redirects neurochemical focus from fear of failure to opportunity for impact, resulting in measurably higher ratings of competence and persuasion.

## 2. The Ego-Centric vs Service-Centric Paradigm

```
                   EGO-CENTRIC MINDSET             SERVICE-CENTRIC MINDSET
             ┌─────────────────────────────┐   ┌─────────────────────────────┐
             │ "How do I look?"            │   │ "What problem am I solving?"│
             │ "What if I mess up?"        │   │ "How can I help this team?" │
             │ "Are they judging my rank?" │   │ "What value can they keep?" │
             └──────────────┬──────────────┘   └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                            ▼                                 ▼
                     Paralysis & Fear                  Freedom & Power
```

### The 3 Core Service Questions
Before speaking, write down answers to these three prompts:
1. *What is the single most valuable insight this audience needs today?*
2. *What mistake or pain can I prevent them from making?*
3. *If they forget my name tomorrow but remember my core solution, have I succeeded?* (The answer must be yes).

## 3. Cognitive Biases That Fuel Speaking Anxiety

| Cognitive Distortion | Reality Check & Mental Antidote |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Spotlight Effect** (Gilovich) | People assume others notice 50% more about their appearance and flaws than they actually do. Audience members are largely preoccupied with their own thoughts, phones, and deadlines. |
| **The Illusion of Transparency** | Speakers assume their internal turmoil is visible. In reality, an audience cannot see your elevated heart rate, dry throat, or stomach butterflies unless you explicitly announce it. |
| **Catastrophizing** | Asking *"What if I freeze?"* Replace with *"If I lose my train of thought, I will pause, smile, look at my outline, and continue. Audiences respect poise, not perfection."* |
