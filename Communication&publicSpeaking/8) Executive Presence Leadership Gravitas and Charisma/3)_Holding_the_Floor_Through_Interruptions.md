# 3) Holding the Floor Through Interruptions

## Executive Overview
In high-stakes corporate and engineering meetings, aggressive peers or dominant executives will inevitably attempt to talk over you, derail your presentation, or steal the floor. Submissive communicators immediately freeze, fall silent, and relinquish control. Combative communicators shout or become visibly emotional. Master leaders hold the floor with calm, surgical poise, enforcing boundaries without ever losing their cool.

## 2. Core Tactics to Hold the Floor

### Tactic 1: The Steamroller (Do Not Stop Speaking)
When someone begins talking while you are speaking:
- **Do not raise your volume** (raising your pitch or shouting signals you feel threatened).
- **Do not speed up** (rushing signals panic).
- **Maintain your exact volume, pace, and downward inflection** for 4 to 6 more words, look directly into the interrupter's eyes, and finish your clause.
- In 70% of cases, the interrupter will realize they collided with an immovable object and back down.

### Tactic 2: The Physical Hand Stop
If the interrupter continues bulldozing:
1. Maintain eye contact with the interrupter.
2. Raise your non-dominant hand smoothly to chest height, with an open palm facing them in a gentle "hold" gesture.
3. Say in a calm, flat, warm tone:
   - *"Give me 30 seconds to finish this thought, and then I want your perspective on it."*
4. Immediately resume your sentence without pausing.
5. Notice you gave them a clear boundary, but dignity: you promised to yield the floor to them after finishing your thought.

### Tactic 3: Reclaiming the Floor for Interrupted Peers
If you see a junior colleague or quieter team member get interrupted by a dominant colleague:
- Step in as the room anchor: *"Hold on, Mark. I was really interested in where Priya was going with that database metric. Priya, finish your point."*
- Instantly establishes you as an empathetic, high-status leader who protects team members and psychological safety.
