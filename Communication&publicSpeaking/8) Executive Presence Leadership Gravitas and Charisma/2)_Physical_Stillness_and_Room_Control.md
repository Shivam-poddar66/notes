# 2) Physical Stillness and Room Control

## Executive Overview
In the animal kingdom and in corporate boardrooms, status is expressed through physical economy of motion. Low-status individuals move constantly: nodding rapidly to gain approval, twitching their hands, adjusting their clothes, touching their faces, or shifting restlessly in their chairs. High-status leaders display profound **physical stillness**. Stillness projects unshakeable calm, emotional self-regulation, and high internal security.

## 2. Table Presence in the Boardroom

Where and how you position yourself physically in an executive meeting directly shapes your perceived authority:

1. **Seating Geography**:
   - Never sit against the back wall or in the "observer row" if you have a seat at the main conference table. Sitting on the perimeter subconsciously labels you as an irrelevant spectator.
   - Sit directly opposite or within the direct eye-line of the key decision-maker (the CEO, CTO, or VP).
2. **Taking Up Physical Space**:
   - Do not pull your elbows tight against your ribs or shrink into a fetal posture.
   - Rest your forearms flat on the conference table in an open V-shape.
   - Claim your territory: place your notebook, pen, and glass of water with deliberate, organized boundaries.
3. **The Still Head Protocol**:
   - When listening to an executive speak, do not nod 40 times like a bobblehead doll. Nodding excessively signals eager desperation for approval.
   - Keep your head still, eyes fixed on their eyes. Offer a single, slow, deliberate nod when they deliver a core insight. This projects that you are critically evaluating their words, not subserviently cheering them on.

---

## 3. Room Control & Spatial Mastery
- When speaking to a room, do not rush to begin. Walk to your spot, plant your feet, look around the room, let the silence settle for 3 seconds, and only then begin speaking.
- Taking ownership of silence and space immediately establishes you as the alpha anchor in the room.
