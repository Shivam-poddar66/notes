# 3) The 3-Second Comprehension Rule and Typography

## Executive Overview
When you switch to a new slide during a talk, the audience will reflexively glance away from your face and scan the screen. If the slide takes more than 3 seconds to decode, you have lost control of their cognitive focus. This chapter codifies the physical rules of **presentation typography**, **pacing**, and the **3-Second Glance Rule**.

---

## 1. The 3-Second Comprehension Rule

```text
  SLIDE TRANSITION ──► AUDIENCE GLANCE (0 - 3s) ──► EYE RETURNS TO SPEAKER
  • Understands main takeaway immediately
  • Slide acts as a visual anchor
  • Speaker's voice remains the dominant channel of information
```

### The Test
Show your slide to a colleague for exactly 3 seconds, then turn off the screen. If they cannot state the core takeaway or point of that slide, the slide must be edited, split into multiple slides, or deleted.

---

## 2. Typography Rules for Presentation Screens

Slides are viewed from distances ranging from 10 feet in a small conference room to 100 feet in an auditorium. Typography that looks readable on a 15-inch laptop display becomes unreadable on a projector.

```text
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                 THE TYPOGRAPHY CEILING                 │
  │                                                        │
  │   SLIDE HEADLINES:       40pt - 60pt Bold Sans-Serif   │
  │   SUBHEADLINES:          28pt - 36pt Semi-Bold         │
  │   BODY / DATA LABELS:    24pt - 28pt Regular           │
  │                                                        │
  │   THE TYPOGRAPHY FLOOR:  24pt (ABSOLUTE MINIMUM)       │
  │   (Anything smaller is a printed document, not a slide)│
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Font Selection Guidelines
- **Use Clean, Modern Sans-Serif Typefaces**:
  - *Recommended*: Inter, SF Pro, Helvetica Neue, Roboto, Montserrat.
  - *Avoid*: Ornate serif fonts, scripts, Comic Sans, or thin hairline weights that wash out under projector glare.
- **Line Length Constraints**:
  - Maximum 6 to 8 words per line.
  - Maximum 3 to 4 lines of text on any single slide.

---

## 3. Guy Kawasaki’s 10/20/30 Presentation Rule

Codified by legendary Apple evangelist Guy Kawasaki for venture capital pitches, this rule remains a gold standard benchmark for business presentations:
- **10 Slides**: The optimal number of concepts an audience can hold in working memory.
- **20 Minutes**: The maximum duration of uninterrupted presentation before audience cognitive fatigue sets in, leaving the remaining time for vibrant Q&A.
- **30-Point Minimum Font**: Forces the presenter to eliminate verbose fluff and feature only the essence of each idea.
