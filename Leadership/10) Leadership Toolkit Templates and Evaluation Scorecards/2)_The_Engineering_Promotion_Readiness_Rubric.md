# 2) The Engineering Promotion Readiness Rubric

## Executive Overview
One of the most contentious moments in an engineering organization is the promotion calibration cycle. If promotions are based on tenure (*"they've been here 2 years"*) or manager favoritism, high performers become disillusioned and leave. Promotions must be governed by an objective rule: **An engineer is promoted only when they have already been consistently performing at the next level for at least 3 to 6 months.** This rubric provides objective behavioral anchors across seniority levels.

---

## 1. The Engineering Career Ladder Spectrum

```text
  [ L3: SOFTWARE ENGINEER ]
  • Scope: Owns well-defined user stories and small features.
  • Independence: Needs pair-programming on complex tasks; delivers with low bug rate.
                   │
                   ▼
  [ L4: SENIOR SOFTWARE ENGINEER ]
  • Scope: Owns end-to-end multi-week epics; designs robust services.
  • Independence: Fully autonomous; leads code reviews; mentors L3 engineers.
                   │
                   ▼
  [ L5: STAFF SOFTWARE ENGINEER ]
  • Scope: Cross-squad architecture; defines multi-quarter technical roadmaps.
  • Independence: Anticipates systemic bottlenecks; unblocks organizational tech debt.
                   │
                   ▼
  [ L6: PRINCIPAL ENGINEER ]
  • Scope: Entire company technical strategy; high-stakes Type 1 architecture.
  • Independence: Directly shapes business strategy and mentors Staff engineers.
```

---

## 2. Senior to Staff Promotion Calibration Rubric

| Competency Dimension | Senior Engineer (L4 Standard) | Staff Engineer (L5 Promotion Standard) | Evidence / Proof |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **System Scope & Complexity** | Designs and builds complex services within a single squad’s domain. | Architects multi-system, cross-squad distributed platforms; solves ambiguous problems. | Authored approved RFCs impacting 3+ squads. |
| **Technical Leadership** | Mentors junior engineers; conducts thorough PR reviews within squad. | Multiplies engineering across the org; runs architecture guilds; raises hiring bar. | Led org-wide adoption of new event-streaming pipeline. |
| **Strategic Thinking** | Executes committed sprint roadmap with high velocity. | Anticipates 12-month technical scaling limits; balances tech debt vs business ROI. | Prevented estimated $200k AWS cost runaway via caching redesign. |
| **Communication & Influence** | Communicates effectively with immediate team and PM. | Persuades Directors and VPs; writes crystal-clear ADRs; diffuses cross-team deadlocks. | Successfully resolved contentious SQL vs NoSQL cross-team dispute. |

## 3. The Promotion Case Packet (The Manager's Weapon)
When presenting a report to the Promotion Committee, the manager must compile:
1. **The Core Thesis**: 1 paragraph summarizing why this promotion is overdue based on sustained next-level performance.
2. **3 Concrete Project Artifacts**: Links to merged RFCs, architecture specs, or incident resolution post-mortems authored by the candidate.
3. **Peer Testimonials (360 Feedback)**: Quotes from cross-functional peers (Product Managers, Tech Leads, SREs) validating their leadership impact.
