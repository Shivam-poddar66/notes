# 3) Continuous Feedback & Radical Candor in Management

## Executive Overview
The traditional corporate ritual of saving feedback for an "Annual Performance Review" is a catastrophic management failure. If an engineer walks into an annual review and hears about a flaw or missed expectation for the first time, the manager has failed. Effective leaders practice **Continuous, Real-Time Feedback** using Kim Scott's **Radical Candor** framework: caring personally while challenging directly.

## 2. The 4 Rules of Masterful Feedback Delivery

### Rule 1: The 48-Hour SLA
Deliver feedback within 24 to 48 hours of the event while context is fresh in working memory. Waiting two weeks turns feedback into ancient history and breeds paranoia.

### Rule 2: Praise in Public, Criticize in Private
- **Praise Publicly**: Specifically call out individual accomplishments during team meetings, Slack channels, and sprint demos.
- **Criticize Privately**: Never reprimand, critique, or tear down an engineer in a group setting. It triggers defensive amygdala fight-or-flight and destroys psychological safety.

### Rule 3: The Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) Model
Eliminate personality evaluations (*"You're being lazy"* or *"You're argumentative"*). Ground feedback in observable facts:
- **Situation**: *"In this morning's sprint planning meeting..."*
- **Behavior**: *"...you interrupted Alex twice while they were walking through their architecture diagram."*
- **Impact**: *"...which caused Alex to lose their train of thought and made junior team members hesitant to share their technical opinions."*

### Rule 4: Praise Specifically, Not Generally
- *Weak*: *"Great job on that project!"* (Meaningless fluff).
- *Strong*: *"The way you documented edge-case handling in section 3 of that RFC saved our QA team at least 10 hours of regression testing. That is the gold standard for our team."*
