# 5) Chapter 5 Summary & Self-Assessment Checklist

## Chapter Summary
- **5 Levels of Delegation**: Calibrate autonomy explicitly from Level 1 (Wait to be told) to Level 5 (Full autonomy). Never delegate without agreeing on the level.
- **RACI & Single Accountability**: Eliminate ambiguous ownership. Enforce the non-negotiable rule: **Only one single 'Accountable' (A) per deliverable**.
- **Task-Relevant Maturity (TRM)**: Calibrate oversight to the engineer's competence in the *specific task*, not their overall corporate seniority.
- **Prevent Reverse Delegation**: Keep the "monkey on their back". Never take back delegated tasks; coach engineers to formulate options and execute their own escalations.
- **Closed-Loop Confirmation**: Have reports reflect back the agreed scope, deadline, and delegation level to eliminate delegation gaps.

---

## 10-Point Self-Assessment Checklist

Score each item from **0 (Never)** to **2 (Consistently Mastered)**:

1. [ ] **Explicit Delegation Levels**: When assigning major tasks, I explicitly define which of the 5 delegation levels applies.
2. [ ] **Single 'A' Enforcement**: In our project plans and RACI matrices, exactly one person is designated as Accountable for each deliverable.
3. [ ] **TRM Task Nuance**: I provide structured guidance when a senior engineer tackles an unfamiliar task for the first time.
4. [ ] **Hands-Off for High TRM**: When an engineer has proven mastery over a domain, I grant full autonomy and avoid checking their code.
5. [ ] **Monkey Defense**: When a report asks me to solve their blocker, I guide them to find options rather than taking the task onto my plate.
6. [ ] **Closed-Loop Alignment**: I ensure reports summarize their next steps and delivery dates in writing or verbally before leaving meetings.
7. [ ] **No Hoarding**: I actively delegate high-visibility, career-defining initiatives rather than keeping them for myself.
8. [ ] **Outcome Oversight**: I inspect delegated deliverables by reviewing outputs and metrics rather than micromanaging the process.
9. [ ] **Failure as Learning**: When a delegated task suffers a setback, I use it as a coaching moment rather than revoking autonomy.
10. [ ] **Scalable Leverage**: My engineering team can execute continuous delivery for two weeks if I am completely out of the office.

**Scoring Guide**:
- **17–20**: Master of delegation, autonomy, and organizational leverage.
- **11–16**: Good delegation instincts; occasional vulnerability to reverse delegation or unclear RACI ownership.
- **0–10**: Severe micromanagement or abdication; team relies entirely on you as an operational bottleneck.
