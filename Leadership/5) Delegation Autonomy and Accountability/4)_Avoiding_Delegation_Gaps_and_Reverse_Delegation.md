# 4) Avoiding Delegation Gaps and Reverse Delegation

## Executive Overview
Two insidious failure modes plague delegation in engineering teams: **The Delegation Gap** (the difference between what the manager thought was delegated and what the employee thought they owned) and **Reverse Delegation** (the employee returning the delegated task back to the manager’s desk). Recognizing and preventing these two dynamics preserves managerial leverage and fosters true employee ownership.

---

## 1. William Oncken’s "Monkey on the Back" Metaphor

In the management classic *Management Time: Who's Got the Monkey?*, William Oncken conceptualized a delegated problem as a "monkey" sitting on an engineer's back.

```text
  REVERSE DELEGATION (THE PATHOLOGY):
  1. Manager delegates database optimization to Engineer.
  2. Engineer hits a snag and slacks Manager: "Hey, the DBA hasn't answered me, what should I do?"
  3. Manager replies: "Let me look into it and ping the DBA for you."
  4. THE MONKEY HAS JUMPED FROM THE ENGINEER'S BACK ONTO THE MANAGER'S BACK!
  5. The manager is now working for their employee, who is waiting on the manager to unblock them!
```

---

## 2. Preventing Reverse Delegation (The 3-Step Defense)

When an engineer attempts to hand a problem back to you:
1. **Never Take the Action Item**:
   - Resist the urge to say *"Let me take care of that for you."*
2. **Shift to Problem-Solving Inquiry**:
   - Ask: *"What are the two best options you have considered for getting the DBA's attention?"*
   - *"If I weren't here today, how would you escalate this blocker?"*
3. **Keep the Monkey on Their Back**:
   - Coach them to draft the escalation email, approve it, and have *them* send it: *"Draft a note to the DBA lead copying me, and let me know if they don't reply by 2 PM."*
   - The engineer remains the owner of the task.

---

## 3. The Delegation Gap & Closed-Loop Confirmation

The Delegation Gap occurs when a manager assigns a task verbally, and both parties walk away with conflicting assumptions about scope, deadline, and authority level.

### The Closed-Loop Confirmation Protocol
Never end a delegation discussion without having the report reflect back:
- *"To make sure we are 100% aligned, how would you summarize our agreed plan, your specific next step, and when we will review progress?"*
- If their summary differs from your expectation, you resolve the misunderstanding in 30 seconds rather than discovering a disaster 3 weeks later.
