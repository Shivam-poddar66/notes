# 3) Building Cross-Organizational Coalitions

## Executive Overview
In organizations larger than 100 people, technical merit alone is insufficient to pass major initiatives (such as a multi-million-dollar cloud migration, re-architecting the core data model, or adopting a new programming framework). If an engineering leader walks into an executive committee meeting and presents a massive proposal that stakeholders are seeing for the first time, it will almost certainly be blocked. Elite leaders build **informal cross-functional coalitions** long before the formal vote.

---

## 1. The Coalition Building Lifecycle

```text
  PHASE 1: STAKEHOLDER MAPPING & LISTENING TOUR (T-minus 4 Weeks)
  • Meet 1-on-1 with key peers (Head of Product, VP of Sales, CFO, CISO).
  • Ask: "What are your biggest bottlenecks with our current platform?"
  • Map out who will gain, who will lose, and who will fear the change.
                          │
                          ▼
  PHASE 2: CO-DESIGNING & TRADING VALUE (T-minus 2 Weeks)
  • Modify your proposal to solve THEIR problems:
    - Add security encryption features to win the CISO's backing.
    - Add real-time reporting to win the VP of Sales's backing.
    - Show hosting cost savings to win the CFO's backing.
                          │
                          ▼
  PHASE 3: THE FORMAL EXECUTIVE MEETING (THE CORONATION)
  • The formal review is NOT for debate; it is the public ratification
    of an agreement that was already negotiated 1-on-1.
```

---

## 2. Stakeholder Influence Matrix

```text
                 HIGH INTEREST / LOW POWER       │       HIGH INTEREST / HIGH POWER
                 [ KEEP INFORMED & ENGAGED ]     │       [ MANAGE CLOSELY / CO-OWN ]
                 • Staff Engineers               │       • VP of Product, CISO, CTO
                 • SRE Team Leads                │       • Critical allies for your coalition
  ───────────────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────
                 LOW INTEREST / LOW POWER        │       LOW INTEREST / HIGH POWER
                 [ MONITOR WITH LOW EFFORT ]     │       [ KEEP SATISFIED / PRE-EMPT ]
                 • External vendor contractors   │       • CFO, General Counsel (Legal)
                 • Peripheral squads             │       • Address compliance & budget risks early
```

---

## 3. The Golden Rule of Political Capital
- Political capital is like money in a bank account: you earn it by delivering on your promises, helping other departments achieve their goals, and solving unglamorous problems.
- Never spend political capital on trivial, vanity technical arguments (e.g. arguing about linters or styling). Save your capital for major architectural pivots that define company survival.
