# 5) Chapter 4 Summary & Self-Assessment Checklist

## Chapter Summary
- **Alignment Cascade**: Connect company Mission $\longrightarrow$ 3-Year Vision $\longrightarrow$ Annual Strategy $\longrightarrow$ Quarterly OKRs $\longrightarrow$ Daily Sprint Tactics. Strategic clarity enables decentralized engineering autonomy.
- **Outcome-Driven OKRs**: Never write task checklists as OKRs. Pair qualitative, ambitious Objectives with quantitative, outcome-based Key Results (measuring latency, uptime, cost, or defect rates).
- **Prioritization Discipline**: Use RICE scoring $(\text{Reach} \times \text{Impact} \times \text{Confidence} / \text{Effort})$ and MoSCoW release gating to make objective, data-backed roadmap decisions.
- **Tech Debt Management**: Treat technical debt like financial leverage. Enforce the 70/20/10 capacity allocation rule and translate refactoring proposals into the currency of Risk, Velocity, and Cost.

---

## 10-Point Self-Assessment Checklist

Score each item from **0 (Never)** to **2 (Consistently Mastered)**:

1. [ ] **Strategic Articulation**: Every engineer on my team can clearly state our top 3 company priorities and how their work connects to them.
2. [ ] **Outcome-Based KRs**: Our Key Results measure business/performance metrics rather than checking off feature shipments.
3. [ ] **Aspirational Scoring**: We calibrate aspirational OKRs so that an average score of 70% reflects aggressive, high-value ambition.
4. [ ] **RICE Prioritization**: We evaluate competing technical initiatives using quantitative frameworks (RICE) rather than gut feel.
5. [ ] **Explicit "Won't Do" Lists**: Our quarterly plans feature a documented list of features and projects we have explicitly chosen *not* to do.
6. [ ] **Protected 20% Tech Debt Budget**: We consistently dedicate ~20% of every sprint capacity to refactoring, tooling, and architectural hygiene.
7. [ ] **Business Translation**: When pitching refactoring, I present financial and operational metrics rather than complaining about code style.
8. [ ] **PM Alignment**: The Engineering Manager and Product Manager jointly agree on roadmap priorities with zero passive-aggressive tension.
9. [ ] **Scope Negotiation**: When deadlines are compressed, we use MoSCoW rules to cut scope rather than mandating forced overtime.
10. [ ] **Trackable Debt Log**: We maintain a transparent technical debt backlog with quantified risk ratings.

**Scoring Guide**:
- **17–20**: Master of strategic execution and technical resource governance.
- **11–16**: Strong strategic alignment; occasional friction balancing product demands against architectural health.
- **0–10**: Highly reactive, feature-factory chaos with compounding technical debt; implement the 70/20/10 rule immediately.
