# 1) Type 1 vs Type 2 Decisions (Jeff Bezos)

## Executive Overview
In his 2015 Letter to Amazon Shareholders, Jeff Bezos articulated a foundational mental model that prevents high-growth tech companies from succumbing to bureaucratic paralysis: distinguishing between **Type 1 (irreversible)** and **Type 2 (reversible)** decisions. Most corporate dysfunction occurs when leaders treat reversible Type 2 decisions as if they were life-or-death Type 1 decisions, subjecting minor features to months of committee review.

---

## 1. The Decision Taxonomy

```text
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │            THE BEZOS DECISION MATRIX         │
                  └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                         │
            ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                                                         ▼
  [ TYPE 1: ONE-WAY DOORS ]                                 [ TYPE 2: TWO-WAY DOORS ]
  • Irreversible or nearly irreversible                     • Reversible, fast, low blast-radius
  • High risk, high financial/system impact                 • Can be walked back easily if wrong
  • Process: Slow, rigorous, deliberate                     • Process: Fast, lightweight, decentralized
  • Examples: Mergers, DB migration, equity, IPO            • Examples: A/B test, UI redesign, MVP feature
```

---

## 2. Comparing Decision Frameworks

| Characteristic | Type 1 Decisions (One-Way Doors) | Type 2 Decisions (Two-Way Doors) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reversibility** | Extremely costly or impossible to undo. | Can be undone in minutes, days, or hours. |
| **Information Required** | 90%+ information, deep benchmarks, security review. | **~70% information** is optimal; waiting for 90% is slow. |
| **Decision Authority** | Executive leadership, Staff Architects, VP/CTO. | Frontline engineers, product squads, tech leads. |
| **Velocity Strategy** | Move deliberately, consult experts, run pre-mortems. | Move fast, run automated experiments, iterate. |
| **Primary Failure Mode** | Moving too fast without analyzing second-order risks. | **Bureaucratic paralysis** (sluggish analysis paralysis). |

---

## 3. The 70% Information Rule
- If you wait until you have 90% or 100% of the information to make a Type 2 decision, you are moving too slowly and your competitors will out-innovate you.
- Make the call with **70% of the data**, and rely on your agility and monitoring to recognize when you made a mistake and reverse course rapidly.
